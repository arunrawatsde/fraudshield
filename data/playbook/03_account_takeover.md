# Account takeover

## What it looks like
An attacker gets into the customer's account (phished password, SIM swap, leaked credentials) and
pays from their own device. They try to drain value fast, buying things that are easy to resell or
move: electronics, gift cards, crypto. They often act at night in the customer's time zone, when the
victim is less likely to notice the alerts.

## Red flags
- First-ever payment from this device.
- Amount many times the customer's median payment (5x or more is strong).
- A category the customer has never used, especially gift cards or crypto exchanges.
- Online channel, often between midnight and 6am.
- A second large payment from the same new device shortly after.

## What is not account takeover
Customers do change phones. After a genuine phone change, the new device keeps paying at the
customer's usual merchants with usual amounts. A new device alone, with normal spending, is not a
takeover. A large purchase on the customer's known device in daytime is also not a takeover.

## Recommended action
New device and large unusual purchase: block, lock the account and contact the customer through a
verified channel. New device with normal amounts: approve and keep monitoring.
