# False positives

## Why they matter
Every wrongly blocked payment is a real customer whose card was declined at a checkout. Too many
false blocks cost more in lost customers and support calls than the fraud they prevent. An
investigator should clear a flag as confidently as they confirm one.

## Common legitimate patterns
- Travel: a flight or hotel booking, then foreign in-store payments hours or days later on the
  usual phone, at everyday merchants and normal amounts.
- One-off big purchase: a laptop, appliance or flight on the customer's known device, at home,
  in daytime, with no follow-up burst.
- New phone: the first payment from a new device, followed by normal spending at familiar
  merchants and amounts.

## How to decide
Look for at least two independent red flags from one pattern before calling something fraud.
One unusual feature with an otherwise normal context should be legitimate, or suspicious if
there is genuine doubt.
